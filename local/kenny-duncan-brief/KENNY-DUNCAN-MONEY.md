## 8. The Nonprofit — Barbers Who Care Inc.

**Direct answer: he does not make money off the nonprofit — and legally he can't.**

- **[VERIFIED]** Real IRS-registered tax-exempt org, **EIN 84-3871680**, Philadelphia ([GuideStar](https://www.guidestar.org/profile/84-3871680)). Note the EIN was issued years after the 2013 start — it ran as an informal volunteer effort first.
- **[VERIFIED]** It files **Form 990-N (e-Postcard)** — the IRS category for orgs with **gross receipts of $50,000 or less per year**. A 990-N contains no financials at all, so there's no salary disclosure — and with under $50K/year total flowing through, there's essentially nothing to draw a salary from.
- **[VERIFIED law]** IRS 501(c)(3) rule: *"No part of the net earnings... may inure to the benefit of any private shareholder or individual."* A founder can take a board-approved *reasonable salary* — which would show on a full 990 — but pocketing nonprofit money is illegal and kills the exemption ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/inurement-private-benefit-charitable-organizations)).
- **[VERIFIED funding]** It runs on **in-kind donations, not cash**: ~30 volunteer barbers donate the labor; **UnitedHealthcare** sponsored supplies (2024, per the Inquirer); **Change Our Future Foundation** (Rodney & Erika McLeod) provided **600 book bags** (2024); Bethesda Project partnership for coat drives/outreach; a Givebutter fundraiser page exists. No government grants found.
- **[ESTIMATE]** The 2024 event's 403 free cuts represent roughly **$10K–16K in donated barber labor** plus sponsor goods — real economic value that never touches the org's bank account, which is why it stays under the 990-N threshold.
- **The real return to Kenny [VERIFIED pattern]:** press (Inquirer, FOX29, CBS, NBC10, 6abc cover it every August), community standing, and brand halo. The nonprofit is the **reputation engine** — it feeds everything else, but it is not an income stream.
